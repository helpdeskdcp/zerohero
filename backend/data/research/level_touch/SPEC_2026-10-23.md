# Level-touch probability (H-LT1) — pre-registered spec, 2026-10-23

Written BEFORE any outcome is computed. Frozen once the first result is printed; any change goes into a dated addendum, never an edit.

## Question
Given a price level L above or below the current price P, how well does a driftless barrier model predict "the 1-minute high/low reaches L within the next 10 minutes"? And does a *structural* level (swing high/low, previous-day high/low, opening-range edge) behave differently from a plain price level at the same distance?
This is about touch probability (mostly volatility), NOT direction. No trade rule, no P&L, no costs in this study.

## Data (read-only)
- NIFTY 50 1-minute, 2015-01 .. 2025 (data/historical/kaggle/mavrick136__...). Primary.
- BANKNIFTY 1-minute, 2015 .. 2024 (sandeepkapri). Independent replication.
- Sessions 09:15-15:30 IST. Bars with volume 0 are kept (index data).

## Periods (fixed now)
- P1 = 2015-01 .. 2019-12, P2 = 2020-01 .. last available. Both must pass independently. No tuning on P2.

## Model (no fitted parameters)
- sigma10(t): EWMA (halflife 30 bars) of squared 1-minute log returns, annualisation not needed, scaled to 10 bars: sigma10 = sqrt(10 * ewma_var). Uses bars up to and including t only.
- Distance d = |L - P| / (P * sigma10). Model: p_hat = min(1, 2 * (1 - Phi(d))). (driftless reflection principle, continuous monitoring; bars are discrete so the model is expected to be slightly high — measured, not corrected.)

## Sampling
- Decision times: every 10th bar from 09:30 to 14:50 IST (so the 10-bar window ends before 15:00 and never crosses the session or a day).
- Probe levels at fixed multiples of sigma10: d in {0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0}, both sides (above = P*(1+d*sigma10), below likewise). Outcome: window high >= L (above) / window low <= L (below), over bars t+1..t+10.
- Structural-level set (separate sample): swing high/low (fractal, N=3 on 5-minute bars, confirmed N bars later, never used before confirmation), previous-day high/low, opening-range (first 15 min) high/low. A decision time is "structural" for a level if the level lies within 0.25..2.0 sigma10 of P at t; its d is its actual distance.

## Metrics (all per period, per instrument)
1. Calibration: bucket p_hat in 10 equal-width bins; reliability error = count-weighted mean |observed - predicted|; calibration slope from a logistic regression of outcome on logit(p_hat) (slope 1 = calibrated).
2. Brier skill score vs the unconditional touch rate of the same period.
3. Level lift: for structural levels, observed touch rate minus the plain-level model prediction at the same d, with a bootstrap 95% CI (resample by day).
4. Reversal-after-touch (descriptive only, no gate): among structural touches, share where price closes back through the level within 5 bars.

## Pass / fail (decided now)
- MODEL OK if, in BOTH periods and BOTH instruments: reliability error <= 0.03 AND calibration slope in [0.8, 1.2]. Otherwise MODEL MISCALIBRATED, and the reported fix is a single monotone recalibration map fitted on P1 and checked on P2 (never fitted on P2).
- LEVEL INFORMATION counts only if the lift CI excludes 0 in the same direction in both periods and both instruments. Otherwise NO LEVEL INFORMATION: structural levels are no better than plain price distance.
- Any study of what to DO with a touch (reversal vs breakout, option payoff, costs) is a separate pre-registered spec.

## Not claimed
No edge, no profitability, no direction forecast. Touch probability is a risk/targets input only. n of days is large here, but option premium, spread and costs are not modelled.
