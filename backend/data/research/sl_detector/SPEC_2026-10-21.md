# Pre-registered spec: stop-loss estimator experiment for Neeraj Joshi's signals (written BEFORE any such signal was seen)

**Purpose.** Neeraj Joshi's channel publishes entry and targets but not the stop ("STOPLOSS :- VIP"). He asked ZeroHero to estimate the stop level, and the project owner will compare the estimate with the real stop. This measures how close a rule-based estimator can get to a human trader's stop. It is an accuracy experiment, NOT a trading engine and NOT a claim about who wins the contest.
**Consent and barrier.** The signal owner asked for this. The estimator is a separate module (`app/sl_detector/`) that sees only (a) the entry/strike/option type/expiry/time of the signal and (b) captured market quotes from BEFORE the post. It writes only to its own hash-chained ledger. No trading module imports it, its output is never used by AUTOSCALP, the ACTIVE arm, H-EV-NG or any live/paper order path, and it is NOT used for the contest score: the contest keeps its fixed default stop (70% of the entry) for posts without a published stop, unless both referees agree otherwise in writing. It is deterministic and rule-based (no learning, no use of any other participant's signals or stops for calibration).
**Method (fixed now, no tuning).** E = entry from the post; t = post time. L = lowest captured last-traded price of the SAME contract in the 60 minutes before t on the same session (if no quote exists: L is missing). Predicted stop = min(0.85*E, max(0.55*E, 0.97*L)); if L is missing or L >= E, L is replaced by the last captured price before t; if there is no quote at all, predicted stop = 0.70*E (and the row is flagged FALLBACK). Rounded to 0.05. The prediction is written to the ledger at ingest time, before the true stop is known to the system.
**Truth.** The real stop is entered later by the project owner (`scripts/sl_detector_truth.py <signal id> <stop>`), into a second hash-chained ledger; an entry with a time earlier than the prediction is rejected.
**Metrics.** For each signal: error = (predicted - true) / E in percentage points of the entry (negative = estimate is wider/lower than the real stop). Reported: n, median |error|, share within +/-5 points and within +/-10 points (with 95% Wilson intervals), share of FALLBACK rows, and, descriptively, the contest outcome of the signal with the true stop versus with the predicted stop (same settlement rules).
**What cannot be claimed.** A "probability that the AI wins" cannot be derived from this: the contest compares net profit of whole participants, this experiment compares one number per signal. With n < 30 every result is labelled INSUFFICIENT_SAMPLE; no profitability or win-probability claim is made. Rounded human stops (e.g. 100, 150) make exact matches meaningless, hence the +/-5 and +/-10 point bands.
