# Constituent lead-lag — FINDINGS 2026-10-25 (spec 373a8c6; scripts/stocks_1m_pull.py + scripts/constituent_leadlag.py; results.json)
Data: Upstox 1-minute, 53 large caps + NIFTY index, 2024-10..2026-10 (10.3M rows, 506 sessions, 4 dropped). Train 2024-10..2025-09, test 2025-10..2026-10.

## Verdict: NO-GO at both horizons (a real but economically negligible effect)
| h | R² own lags only | R² full (heavy+rest+own) | gain | perm p | trades | gross bp | net bp @1.5 | CI lo |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 min | 0.00077 | 0.00214 | +0.0014 | <0.0005 | 5,380 | +0.24 | -1.26 | -1.40 |
| 5 min | -0.00001 | 0.00037 | +0.0004 | <0.0025 | 3,512 | +0.16 | -1.34 | -1.65 |
- Heavyweights do carry a statistically detectable lead (permutation p ~ 0), but the explained variance is 0.1-0.2 % of 1-minute index variance: gross +0.2 bp per trade against a 1.5 bp cost.
- It is NOT the heavyweights that carry the lead: heavy+own R² 0.0013 < rest+own 0.0027 (h=1). The broad stock basket leads the index slightly more than the ten biggest names — no sign of "money in heavyweights moves the index" as something predictable ahead of time.
- Index = arithmetic of stocks, so the 1-minute lag found is the small non-synchronous-trading effect, at the edge of what 1-minute candles can show; tick data could show more lead, but anything that small is below cost for retail.
Conclusion with test A/B of operator_tests: no measurable footprint of a hidden steering mechanism in index-vs-constituent behaviour; the little predictability that exists is far below transaction cost. Observation only, nothing wired. Data kept at data/historical/stocks_1m (reusable).
